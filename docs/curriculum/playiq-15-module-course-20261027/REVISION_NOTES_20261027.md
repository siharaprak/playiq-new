# PlayIQ course revision notes - October 27, 2026

## Approved structure

The course contains 15 sections total: Module 00 plus Modules 1-14. Module
14 is the Capstone and the last section. No extra Capstone follows it.

The approved content sequence is:

1. Preserve the existing Module 00 and Modules 1-8 curriculum, including the
   Module 1 character and guardrail tutorial and light character connections
   already placed in Modules 2-4.
2. Keep Module 9 as one continuous, start-to-finish tutorial that compiles
   six files and applies the Tutor build in the same parent-approved Project
   started in Module 00.
3. Move the Tutor's six actual behavior tests, test-result review, one-change
   improvement, and “new idea to learning” loop into Module 10.
4. Use Module 11 to build the student's own Personal Assistant in its own
   separate Project. Use Module 12 to test it and clarify what it can and
   cannot do. Do not make either module an assistant build for another person.
5. Make Module 13 a short, optional possibility preview showing how the
   existing Character Guardrail Card could lead to a story, storyboard, and
   cartoon short later. It is not a required build, assignment, artifact, or
   submission.
6. Make Module 14 the Master Trial Capstone: performance preparation,
   evidence review, Learning Dashboard tutorial, one small chosen skills
   demonstration, final Tutor record review, family review, graduation, and
   future-course preview.

## Evidence boundary: learning preferences

PlayIQ's learning design lets students notice preferences and test which
approaches help them engage, explain, retrieve, and apply a particular topic.
It must not present “visual,” “auditory,” or other fixed learning-style
categories as proven diagnoses, or promise that matching teaching to a
category will always make a student learn faster.

This caution reflects mixed research evidence. Pashler et al. explain that
preferences exist, but preference alone does not establish that instruction
matching a learner category improves outcomes; the proposed matching effect
requires a particular experimental interaction that the review found largely
missing. The 2024 meta-analysis reported a small overall positive effect but
noted that only 26% of outcome measures showed the crossover pattern needed
to support the matching claim and concluded that evidence remained
inconclusive. The student-facing course therefore treats preferences as
changeable hypotheses and checks actual work rather than claiming a fixed
learning type.

Sources:

- Pashler, McDaniel, Rohrer, and Bjork, “Learning Styles: Concepts and
  Evidence,” *Psychological Science in the Public Interest* (2008):
  https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1539-6053.2009.01038.x
- Clinton-Lisell and Litzinger, “Is it really a neuromyth? A meta-analysis of
  the learning styles matching hypothesis,” *Frontiers in Psychology* (2024):
  https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1428732

## Reusable course-authoring learnings

Carry these lessons into future PlayIQ course drafting and review:

- Begin with a quick, exciting, low-friction hook that lets the student
  picture a possibility before introducing terminology or a difficult test.
- Give students a clear early win with exact steps: what account or workspace
  to open, where to go, what to paste, what they should see, and what to do
  next. Do not make them guess where an instruction belongs.
- Keep a coherent, start-to-finish build together. If testing needs its own
  module, preserve the completed build flow and make the next module a
  deliberately separate test-and-improve phase.
- Teach why the student is building the AI tool and what the instructions,
  knowledge files, evidence, and safety boundaries each do. Do not make the
  core work feel like unexplained copy-paste.
- Separate required work from an exciting future possibility. A story,
  storyboard, animation, or next-course idea may be a short seed rather than
  another assignment.
- Keep each module substantive but legible: teach in short chunks, give
  concrete examples, show how to answer, and tell students what happens after
  each step. Use checkpoints to improve pacing rather than deleting useful
  teaching or interrupting a coherent tutorial.
- Treat personal learning preferences as hypotheses to investigate with
  actual practice and retrieval evidence. Do not convert a preference into a
  permanent label or guaranteed advantage.
- Keep the Parent Dashboard and course site honest about account boundaries;
  copy/paste directions do not imply that the site can read an external AI
  workspace.
