## Part 1: A tiny map of a future project

If you choose to make this kind of project after PlayIQ, the path could be as
simple as these four steps. Read the steps now; do not start the project as
part of this course.

### 1. Bring your character guardrail

Open the Character Guardrail Card you made in Module 1. It describes the
details you want to stay consistent, such as shape, colors, clothing,
personality, and what should not change.

### 2. Imagine one short story

Write a beginning, a challenge, and an ending. Ask your AI to help you explore
options, but choose the story yourself. Keep it small: one character, one
place, one problem, one change.

### 3. Turn the story into a storyboard

Break the story into a few moments, like comic panels. For each moment, note
what the character is doing, where the scene happens, and what the viewer
should notice. Review each panel against your character guardrail.

### 4. Make a short cartoon

Use an age-appropriate, parent-approved tool to create or animate the scenes.
Make one scene at a time, compare it to the guardrail, and revise anything
that drifts. Your decisions guide the AI; you remain the creator and editor.

That is the whole idea: **character guardrail -> story -> storyboard ->
cartoon short**. You do not need to make any of it now.

## Part 2: Why the guardrail helps

When an AI makes a new image or scene, it may change details you wanted to
keep. A guardrail card gives you a checklist for asking:

- Does the character still have the details I chose?
- Does this scene fit the story I want to tell?
- What changed that I want to correct?
- What new choice do I want to add to my instructions?

A guardrail helps explain what you want; it cannot guarantee identical images
every time. You still compare, choose, and revise. This is the same pattern
you learned across PlayIQ: describe the goal, give clear instructions, check
the result, and make a deliberate improvement.
