# PlayIQ Module 11: Build Your Personal AI Assistant

## Your next unlock: make a useful helper for your own day

Would it be useful to turn one annoying, repetitive task into a clear
assistant plan? Today you get to design a helper around something you choose.

Your Tutor is built to help you learn. Now imagine a different kind of
helper: one that supports a small task you choose, such as planning a creative
project, organizing a practice routine, or turning a list of ideas into a
clear checklist.

The secret is not asking AI to “do everything.” It is describing one real
workflow clearly: what starts the task, what information is needed, what steps
happen, what a useful result looks like, and what must stay under your
control. Then you will use discovery questions to shape a separate Personal
Assistant Project.

**By the end of this module, I can map one safe task, use discovery questions
to define an assistant, and build a separate Personal Assistant Project with
clear instructions and boundaries.**

**Skill Tree:** Workflow Designer  
**Mission:** Map one task, discover the requirements, and build the assistant.

## Part 1: Tutor or assistant? Choose the right tool

Your **Tutor Project** is for learning: explaining, asking, hinting, checking,
and helping you practice. Your **Personal Assistant Project** will support
one task you choose. Keep them separate so each Project has a clear purpose.

| Your Tutor | Your Personal Assistant |
|---|---|
| Helps you understand and practice | Helps with one chosen task |
| Uses approved learning notes and rules | Uses only task information you choose to provide |
| Helps preserve your thinking and authorship | Helps organize or prepare; you review and decide |
| Stays in the Tutor Project from Module 00 | Goes in a new, separate private Project |

Examples of a safe first assistant task:

- turn a creative idea into a checklist;
- help plan a practice session using time you choose;
- organize a personal reading list;
- help prepare materials for a hobby project;
- turn your own notes into a next-step list.

Choose something small enough to describe and check. Do not choose a task
about health treatment, money decisions, passwords, private family matters,
school cheating, or contacting other people.

## Part 2: Map the real workflow before building

A **workflow** is the steps that take a task from its beginning to a useful
result. Think about a real task you do now. You are not automating it; you are
learning to describe it clearly.

### Step 1 — Name the task

### Step 2 — Write the steps you already follow

```text
WHAT STARTS THE TASK?

WHAT INFORMATION DO I NEED?
1.
2.
3.

WHAT STEPS DO I TAKE?
1.
2.
3.

WHAT RESULT WOULD HELP ME?

WHAT SHOULD I CHECK MYSELF?
```

### Step 3 — Spot the decision points

Where do you make a choice? What happens if the information is missing, the
plan changes, or an unexpected problem appears? Write those branches down.

If ______________________________, then I usually __________________________

If information is missing, I want the assistant to ________________________

If my plan changes, I want the assistant to ________________________________

This is how you move from “make me an assistant” to a job description the AI
can follow.

## Part 3: Use discovery questions to learn what you actually want

You do not have to invent perfect instructions in one try. Ask the AI to help
you discover what the assistant needs to know. It must ask only one question
at a time and show you its understanding before drafting instructions.

With your parent or guardian, open a **new private chat** in the parent-
approved AI account. Do not put these instructions in your Tutor Project.
Paste this discovery prompt:

```text
Help me design a personal AI assistant for one safe task I choose.

First, interview me one question at a time. Ask only what you need to learn:
1. What exact task do I want help with?
2. What starts the task, and when do I want help?
3. How do I do it now, step by step?
4. What information can I safely provide?
5. What result or format would be useful?
6. Where do I need to make the decision myself?
7. What should the assistant do when information is missing or plans change?
8. What must it never assume, store, send, decide, or do?
9. How will I check that the assistant helped?

Do not ask for passwords, financial details, private family information,
health information, or information about another person. Do not connect to
accounts, send messages, make purchases, set real reminders, or take actions.
After the interview, show me a short Assistant Brief with the task, inputs,
steps, output, decision points, boundaries, and a test idea. Tell me what is
still unclear. Wait for my approval before drafting any Project Instructions.
```

Answer with only information you are comfortable sharing. You can skip a
question or ask your parent for help. If the AI asks for something private,
do not provide it; remind it of the prompt's limits.

## Part 4: Turn the brief into clear instructions

When the brief is accurate, ask the AI to draft instructions. You remain the
designer. Read them, remove anything you do not want, and ask for a revision
if they are unclear.

Copy this prompt into the same private assistant-design chat:

```text
Using only the Assistant Brief I approved, draft a short set of Project
Instructions. Include:

1. Role and one-sentence purpose.
2. How to start each task and what to ask if key information is missing.
3. The steps the assistant may help me organize or prepare.
4. The output format I requested.
5. What I must review or decide myself.
6. Privacy, permission, accuracy, and safety boundaries.
7. A note that the assistant must not claim to save information, send
   messages, set reminders, or take outside actions unless the platform shows
   that exact capability and I approve it.

Keep the instructions short and specific. Do not invent facts, user needs,
knowledge, or platform features. Show me the draft and wait for my approval.
```

### Instruction review card

Check each statement before using it:

- [ ] It describes one task, not “everything.”
- [ ] It says what information the assistant needs and what it should do when
      something is missing.
- [ ] It describes a useful result in a format I understand.
- [ ] It leaves important choices and final approval to me.
- [ ] It does not request or store unnecessary private information.
- [ ] It does not pretend to have tools or memory that I have not confirmed.
- [ ] I understand every instruction and agree with it.

## Part 5: Create the separate Personal Assistant Project

Do this with a parent or guardian:

Use the platform steps from Module 00, but give this **separate** tool the
Assistant name below. ChatGPT keeps reference files in Sources, Claude's
documented chat layout uses project knowledge, and an available Gemini Gem
uses Knowledge; behavior instructions go in the **instructions editor**, not
just a file upload. Claude labels its save **Save instructions**; a Gemini
preview still needs **Save**. Reopen to check. These routes are **official-doc
verified, not account tested**. Module 00 links the official help; use its
existing fallback if this account's controls differ.

1. Sign in to the AI account approved for you.
2. Create a **new private Project/workspace** called `My Personal Assistant`
   or a name you and your parent choose.
3. Keep it separate from the Tutor Project you started in Module 00.
4. Add the approved Project Instructions in that tool's **instructions editor**
   and save there, using the Module 00 platform guide. Reopen and check the
   exact wording; a chat reply or reference upload is not enough. Do not share
   your password with Orion or anyone in the course.
5. If the platform has an approved knowledge-file area and your task truly
   needs a reference file, use a short file made from information you are
   allowed to include. Ask your parent to review it first. Do not upload a
   book, private document, or another person's information without permission.
6. If your platform does not support Projects or saved instructions, keep the
   brief and instruction set in a parent-approved document and paste only the
   needed text into a private chat when you choose. Do not buy a new plan.

**Important:** this creates a configured AI helper, not a background service.
Creating a Project does not make it send messages, monitor accounts, or
remember information outside the platform's actual features.

## Part 6: What belongs in the assistant's instructions or knowledge?

Instructions tell the assistant **how to behave**. A knowledge file gives it
**specific reference information** for its task. Use the smallest amount of
information that makes the assistant useful.

| Put this in instructions | Put this in a knowledge file only if needed |
|---|---|
| Purpose and task boundaries | A checklist you created for the task |
| Steps and output format | A short glossary or project reference |
| How to ask when something is missing | A public or parent-approved reference |
| What you review and decide | Your own notes you choose to use |

Do not put a rule in a knowledge file and expect the AI to follow it as an
instruction. Do not put private details into a file just because the platform
allows uploads. Your parent helps decide what is appropriate.

If no reference file is necessary, write **“No knowledge file needed yet.”**
That is a good design decision, not missing work.
