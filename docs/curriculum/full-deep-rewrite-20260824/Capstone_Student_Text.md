# PlayIQ Capstone: The Master Trial

## Your final mission: learn it, perform it, build it, and own it

Look at what you can do now.

You can ask a better question. You can pre-learn a hard topic, find a missing
connection, compress information, quiz yourself, learn from mistakes, organize
knowledge, protect your voice, build a personal tutor, and design for a real
user.

Now you will connect those powers.

The Master Trial is not one giant exam and it is not a victory lap made of
checkmarks. You will use the full PlayIQ system on one real learning challenge,
complete a performance event, create something that matters to you, prepare a
Learning Dashboard, finalize your tutor files, and explain the evidence to your
parent or guardian.

**By the end of this Capstone, I can independently plan, learn, perform, create,
verify, and improve—with AI supporting my thinking rather than replacing it.**

Your final portfolio will contain:

1. an Exam & Performance Plan;
2. a before-and-after performance evidence set;
3. one selected creation-track build;
4. a Learning Dashboard;
5. a final Personal Tutor Project Pack;
6. a Parent Review Packet;
7. a student ownership statement and next plan.

## Part 2: The Exam & Performance System

The older Exam & Performance System now lives inside the Master Trial. It is
the sequence you use to prepare, perform, and learn from the result.

### The six-stage performance loop

1. **Define** — understand what the performance actually requires.
2. **Map** — pre-learn the topic and locate the hard parts.
3. **Rescue** — repair missing connections.
4. **Practise** — retrieve, apply, explain, and use the Mistake Bank.
5. **Perform** — complete a timed or realistic attempt without hidden help.
6. **Review** — compare evidence and choose the next improvement.

This is not cheating. Preparation makes the real event feel more familiar.
The real advantage is learning how to learn sooner, with less wasted effort,
while still doing the thinking and practice yourself.

## Part 3: Active comparison — first attempt vs. prepared attempt

### Why this experiment matters

You need an honest comparison to decide which learning actions helped. You do
not need an invented PDI score or a fixed improvement target.

Use two comparable tasks on the same topic:

- **Attempt A — starting snapshot:** complete a short task before the full
  performance loop.
- **Attempt B — prepared performance:** complete a different but comparable
  task after using the loop.

Do not repeat the exact same questions and call memory "improvement."

### Honest interpretation

- More correct work may show stronger understanding, but check task difficulty.
- Fewer hints may show independence, but only if the tasks were comparable.
- Faster work is useful only when accuracy and reasoning remain strong.
- Higher confidence is useful only when it matches checked performance.
- One disappointing attempt is a clue for the next plan, not proof that the
  learning method or student cannot improve.

No approved PDI formula is used here. If PlayIQ later provides an approved
calculation, it must show its formula, source data, and limitations before you
or your parent relies on it.

Required pieces:

- problem and user;
- input checklist;

Required pieces:

- rules and choices;

- player feedback;
- next prototype change.

AI may brainstorm possibilities. You choose, combine, test, and explain the

### Track 3 — Physical Object Dream Creator

Turn an imagined object into a researched concept and prototype plan.

Required pieces:

- problem the object addresses;
- user and setting;
- labelled sketch;
- size, materials, and key features;
- safety and feasibility questions;
- source-backed research notes;
- paper, cardboard, model, or digital prototype plan.

Do not claim that an untested design is safe, manufacturable, or ready to sell.

### Track 4 — AI Chef

Create a recipe, meal, or food-planning assistant for an approved user.

Required pieces:

- user's goal and preferences;
- parent-confirmed allergy and safety boundary;
- available ingredients or constraints;
- assistant instructions;
- one generated plan or recipe;
- adult review of food safety and suitability;
- taste/usefulness feedback and revision.

AI does not replace medical advice or adult food-safety judgment.

### Track 5 — Personal Expense Assistant

Required pieces:

- fictional or approved sample data;
- summary and question workflow;

- one test and revision.

The assistant does not access accounts, move money, recommend investments, or
make financial decisions.

## Part 7: Build the Learning Dashboard

### Why this matters

A dashboard is not decoration and it is not a machine that declares how smart
you are. It organizes evidence so you can notice patterns and decide what to do
next.

Your dashboard may be a table, spreadsheet, sketch, presentation, or app
prototype. It must remain human-readable.

### The five questions your dashboard should answer

1. What have I studied or practised?
2. Where does checked performance match or disagree with my confidence?
3. Which mistakes or missing connections repeat?
4. Which learning methods helped under which conditions?
5. What should I practise next, and why?

### Required data record

```yaml
- date: [date]
  topic: [topic]
  activity: [what was attempted]
  source: [module, worksheet, approved test, or student reflection]
  confidence_before: [1-5 or not_recorded]
  checked_result: [actual result or not_checked]
  mistake_or_gap: [observation or none_observed]
  method_tested: [method]
  help_needed: [none | hint | several_hints | other]
  next_step: [specific practice]
  evidence_status: [observed | student_reported | experiment_result | missing]
```

Use `not_recorded`, `not_checked`, or `missing` when data does not exist. Do not
fill empty spaces with guesses.
