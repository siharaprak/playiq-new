# PlayIQ Module 3: The Pre-Learn System

## Your mission: make tomorrow's lesson feel familiar today

Imagine walking into class and hearing a new topic—but instead of feeling lost,
you already recognize the main idea, the important words, and the question you
want to ask.

That is pre-learning.

You are not trying to master the entire lesson before the teacher teaches it.
You are giving your brain a map so the new details have somewhere to land.

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

- turn a large topic into a five-part map;
- find the simplest core idea;
- compare map-first and example-first learning;
- create a ten-line pre-learn brief;
- choose a testable pre-learn setting for your future tutor;
- arrive in class with less overwhelm and better questions.

Estimated time: 40 to 55 minutes.

## Part 1 - Why pre-learning destroys some overwhelm

Overwhelm often arrives when your brain receives too many unfamiliar pieces
at once. A pre-learn session reduces the number of surprises.

The goal is familiarity, not perfection.

### The five-part map

A useful starter map answers:

1. What is the topic?
2. What are its main parts?
3. Which words will probably appear?
4. What is one easy example?
5. What should I listen for or ask in class?

### Example: photosynthesis

**Topic:** How plants turn light energy into stored chemical energy.

**Five parts:**

1. sunlight;
2. water;
3. carbon dioxide;
4. glucose;
5. oxygen.

**Starter question:** Where inside the plant does this happen?

Notice what the map does not do: it does not include every chemical reaction,
historical discovery, and exception. It gives the brain an entrance.

### First principle: the idea underneath the details

A first principle is the simplest useful idea that helps the other details
make sense.

For photosynthesis:

> Plants use light energy to build food from water and carbon dioxide.

That sentence is not the entire lesson. It is the hook that the details attach
to.

## Part 4 - Non-examples reveal the boundary

An example shows what a concept is. A non-example shows what it is not. Seeing
both helps the brain find the boundary.

Example concept: **mammal**

- Example: dolphin—warm-blooded, breathes air, nurses its young.
- Non-example: shark—fish with gills; it does not nurse its young.

## Part 6 - Learning Supercharger Blueprint update

This setting tells your future tutor how to introduce a **brand-new topic**.
It does not control every explanation and it can change when evidence changes.

Examples:

- `map-first`: begin with the main parts, then show examples;
- `example-first`: begin with examples and a non-example, then show the map;
- `question-first`: first ask what the student already knows, then choose the
